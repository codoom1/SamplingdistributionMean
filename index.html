Sampling Distribution of the Mean

Explanation

This simulation demonstrates the Central Limit Theorem (CLT) using test scores.

The Sampling Process:

  1. We start with a population of test scores with mean μ and standard deviation σ.
  2. We randomly select 30 individuals from this population.
  3. We calculate the mean of this sample.
  4. We repeat this process 100 times.
  5. The histogram shows the distribution of these sample means.

Key observations:

  • The histogram shows the distribution of sample means.
  • The red curve represents the theoretical normal distribution.
  • As you increase the number of repeated samples, the histogram more closely matches the normal curve.
  • The mean of the sample means (x̄) approaches the population mean (μ).
  • The standard deviation of the sample means approaches σ/√n.
  • For non-normal distributions: The sampling distribution approaches normality as sample size increases, especially when n ≥ 30.
